# Ten-Thousand-Lesson Curriculum Evidence Boundary

## Curriculum Anchor

The 10,000-lesson architecture will use the six CEFR levels—A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, and C2—as its progression framework. The Council of Europe describes the levels through progressive, skill-related “can-do” descriptors and groups them as Basic, Independent, and Proficient users.[1] The curriculum will therefore model capability growth rather than claim that an accumulated lesson count alone establishes proficiency.

Portuguese as a Foreign Language certification through CAPLE also assesses competence from A1 to C2. Its stated use cases include study, academic pathways, work, and nationality-related requirements, which confirms the need for personal, academic, civic, and professional situation domains.[2]

## Four-Skill Contract

Every lesson record must have an explicit learner action for the following skills:

| Skill | Required learner evidence |
|---|---|
| Listening | Hear a Portuguese input and make a comprehension, selection, or sequencing decision. |
| Reading | Read an appropriate text and identify purpose, detail, inference, or action. |
| Writing | Compose, transform, or repair a Portuguese response for a defined audience and context. |
| Speaking | Produce an oral response, rehearse a model, or complete a role-play. Browser voice playback supports modelling; spoken assessment must not be claimed without an assessment workflow. |

## Originality and Research Boundary

External sources are used only to identify authoritative proficiency frameworks and real-world domains. The platform will create original prompts, dialogues, tasks, vocabulary selections, and assessment items. It will not scrape, reproduce, or represent third-party course texts, instructor materials, or proprietary test content as Prize2Pride curriculum.

## Editorial Boundary

Deterministic lesson metadata can scale to 10,000 records, but generated or authored learner-facing material remains a **draft** until its facts, regional Portuguese choices, Arabic/Tunisian explanations, task level, and accessibility have passed an editorial review process.
